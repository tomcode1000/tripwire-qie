# Tripwire: Flow image and video brief

Six images and two short loops for the Tripwire website. Each prompt is written to be pasted into Flow as-is.
Generate 4 variations of each, keep the best one, and save it under the exact file name given, in `web/img/`.

## The rules for every prompt (already written into each one)

The site is an institution's security desk, not a crypto landing page. Every image is a **real, physical
object photographed like evidence or like a museum catalogue**: one subject, quiet light, real materials,
real imperfections, large calm negative space for the headline.

Rejected on sight, in every image:
- anything that looks like a template, stock photo, 3D render, illustration, or "AI art"
- glowing neon, holograms, circuit boards, binary code, blockchain cubes, chains, padlock icons, shields
- hooded hackers, people, hands with perfect skin, faces
- screens, UI, logos, letters, numbers, watermarks, text of any kind
- bokeh orbs, lens flares, purple-blue gradients, plastic-looking metal, over-sharpened HDR
- symmetrical "hero product" staging, floating objects, dramatic smoke

Palette to match the site: warm paper white (#F4F3EF), graphite and ink black (#1B1A1F), brushed steel,
aged brass, a single deep indigo (#3D36C9) only where stated, and one signal red (#B3261E) only where stated.

## 1. Hero, light theme: `hero-light.jpg` (16:9, at least 2560×1440)

> Editorial still-life photograph, shot like a plate in a museum catalogue of security objects. A single
> hair-thin steel tripwire is stretched taut and perfectly straight across a pale limestone surface, running
> from the right edge of the frame towards the centre, where it is held by one small aged-brass anchor pin
> driven into the stone. The wire catches a thin line of daylight along its length. Everything else is
> calm, empty, warm paper-white stone with natural pores and faint mineral veining. Camera: medium-format,
> 120mm macro lens, low angle about 15 degrees above the surface, shallow depth of field so the far end of
> the wire softens gently; the brass pin is pin-sharp. Light: soft north-window daylight from the upper
> right, long gentle shadow of the wire cast across the stone. Colour: warm off-white and pale grey stone,
> muted brass, steel. Composition: the left 55 percent of the frame is clean empty stone for a headline;
> subject sits in the right third. Real texture, real dust specks, honest imperfection. No text, no logos,
> no people, no hands, no screens, no glowing effects, no neon, no 3D render look, no illustration, no
> stock-photo staging, no lens flare, no bokeh orbs, no gradients. It must look like a real photograph a
> serious institution would commission. Reject anything that looks like a template or AI art.

## 2. Hero, dark theme: `hero-dark.jpg` (16:9, at least 2560×1440)

> Same object and composition as a night-time version: a single hair-thin steel tripwire stretched taut
> across a dark honed basalt surface, held by one small aged-brass anchor pin in the right third of the
> frame. A single narrow beam of cool light grazes the stone from the right, so only the wire, the pin and
> a thin strip of stone texture are lit; the rest falls into deep graphite black with readable texture,
> never crushed. Medium-format, 120mm macro, low angle, shallow depth of field. The left 55 percent of the
> frame is dark, quiet negative space for a headline. Colours: graphite, basalt black, steel, muted brass.
> Real photograph, real stone pores, faint dust in the light. No text, no logos, no people, no hands, no
> screens, no neon, no glow, no smoke, no lens flare, no bokeh orbs, no 3D render look, no illustration.
> Reject anything template-like or AI-looking.

## 3. The vault's delay: `timelock.jpg` (4:5, at least 1600×2000)

> Close documentary photograph of a real antique bank-vault time lock: three mechanical clock movements
> mounted side by side on a brushed-steel plate inside the back of an open vault door, the kind made by
> Sargent & Greenleaf or Yale in the early twentieth century, with enamel dials, blued-steel hands and
> visible brass gears. Shot straight on but slightly off-axis, 90mm lens, f/5.6, everything sharp. Light:
> soft overhead museum lighting with one warm practical light from the left, gentle falloff into a dark
> steel surround. Patina, fine scratches, fingerprints wiped off but traces remain, real age. Colours:
> brushed steel, ivory enamel, blued steel, warm brass. No readable brand names, no text, no numbers that
> read clearly, no people, no hands. Not a 3D render, not an illustration, not stock. It must feel like a
> plate from a book about the history of bank security.

## 4. Where it leaked: `leak.jpg` (3:2, at least 2400×1600)

> Overhead flat-lay photograph on a matte warm-grey archival board: a single plain manila evidence
> envelope lies slightly open, and from it one thin bright red thread (#B3261E) runs out across the board in
> a straight, deliberate line and exits the frame on the right. Beside the envelope, a small brass key tag
> with no writing. Nothing else. Shot from directly above with a 50mm lens, even soft daylight, very
> slight shadow under the envelope's edge. Real paper fibres, a faint crease, an honest worn corner.
> Generous empty board space on the left half. No text on the envelope or tag, no stamps, no logos, no
> people, no hands. Not a render, not an illustration, no stock-photo look, no vignette, no colour grading
> tricks. Quiet and exact, like an evidence photograph in an institutional report.

## 5. The registry: `ledger.jpg` (16:9, at least 2560×1440)

> Photograph of the edge of a closed, heavy archival ledger book on a dark oak reading desk: we see the
> stack of cream page edges in sharp detail, a dark green cloth spine and worn leather corners. One single
> thin indigo (#3D36C9) ribbon bookmark hangs out of the pages. Raking soft daylight from the left
> reveals paper texture along the page block. 100mm macro, f/4, focus on the page edges, gentle falloff.
> The right half of the frame is soft, dark, quiet wood for text. No visible writing, no text, no
> numbers, no logos, no people, no hands, no candles, no smoke. Not a render, not an illustration, not
> stock. Feels like a national archive's reading room.

## 6. Social card: `og.jpg` (1200×630)

> Use the light hero photograph (image 1) cropped so the brass anchor pin and the taut wire sit in the
> right third and the left two thirds are clean pale stone. No added text: the site places the title.

## Video loops (Flow, image-to-video from the stills above)

Keep both under 6 seconds, seamless loop, no camera cuts, export MP4 H.264 1920×1080 and keep the file under 4 MB.

### A. `hero-light.mp4` / `hero-dark.mp4` (from images 1 and 2)

> Locked-off camera, absolutely no camera movement. The only motion: a very slow line of light travels
> along the taut wire from right to left, as if daylight drifted across it, and the faintest, almost
> imperceptible tension tremor in the wire near the brass pin, once, then still. Dust specks drift very
> slowly in the light. Everything else is perfectly still. Natural, physical, subtle; nothing glows,
> nothing sparkles, no particles bursting, no zoom, no parallax, no added objects. Seamless loop.

### B. `timelock.mp4` (from image 3)

> Locked-off camera, no movement. The three clock movements' second hands advance with real mechanical
> ticks, the balance wheels oscillate, light stays constant. Nothing else moves. No zoom, no glow, no
> added objects. Seamless 5-second loop.
