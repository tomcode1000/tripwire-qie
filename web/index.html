How a trap fires

The bot's speed is what catches it.

Scammers use honeypot wallets to rob the greedy. Tripwire turns the trick around: the bait is the alarm, and a bot that grabs it first warns you while your real key is still safe.

  1. 01

    Plant

    Tripwire makes bait keys that look like a real deploy key, each showing a 500 rUSD balance. Put them where a real key could leak: a repo's .env, notes, CI secrets. Each is labelled with where it went.

  2. 02

    Wait

    Nothing happens until someone uses a bait key. Planting costs nothing on QIE: a transfer is about $0.000004 and the whole system deploys for 0.0042 QIE.

  3. 03

    Trip

    rUSD never leaves a bait wallet. Any transfer, sweep or approval calls the trap instead, in the thief's own transaction. Native-coin sweeps are reported by the keeper.

  4. 04

    Respond

    Your vault locks with the leak's location as the reason. The thief joins the public list. Funds can now only go to your recovery address.

The vault

What a thief can do with your keys

Your real funds sit in a TripwireVault: a small daily amount goes out instantly, anything larger waits 24 hours and can be cancelled, and every security setting belongs to your cold recovery key.

The 24-hour window

The honest limit: if the thief never touches bait, no trap fires. A big withdrawal still waits 24 hours, so a builder who checks once cancels it and loses at most the daily limit.

What Tripwire does not do

Status on —: contracts live on QIE testnet, 65 tests passing, not audited. Mainnet deployment is next.

  • A thief who never touches the baitNo trap fires. The vault's 24-hour delay is your protection, so keep real funds in the vault.
  • Bait wallets must hold no real valuerUSD can't move, but anything real you put in a bait wallet can be swept like any other wallet.
  • The keeper is a watcher you runIt can only lock and flag, never move funds. Token trips need no keeper at all.
  • Bait wallets are tools, not usersWe do not count them as traction.
